## First meeting checklist
The first meeting sets the tone for the whole group, so keep it short and practical. Start with quick introductions: what each person is studying, their current level and what they hope to get out of the group. Agree on a goal for the next few weeks, such as finishing a past paper each week or covering one chapter per session. Decide how often and when you will meet, and pick a regular slot so people can plan around it. Choose one place for shared notes and messages so nothing gets lost. Agree on simple ground rules, such as arriving prepared and letting the group know early if you cannot attend. End by writing down who is doing what before the next session. A group that leaves the first meeting with a clear goal, a schedule and an owner for each task is far more likely to keep going.

## Roles in a group
Small roles keep sessions running smoothly and stop the whole load falling on one person. A facilitator keeps time and makes sure the agenda is followed. A note-taker records key points and decisions and shares them afterwards. A questioner prepares a few tough questions in advance to test everyone's understanding. A summariser closes each session by restating what was covered and what is next. Rotate the roles every week so everyone practises explaining, listening and organising. In a group of three or four people, one person can hold two roles. The owner of the group on SG Match accepts new members and can edit the group's details, but day-to-day leadership works best when it is shared, so roles should not depend on one person being present.

## Keeping momentum
Most study groups fade because the meetings become irregular, not because the people lose interest. Protect a fixed weekly time and treat it like a class. Start each session by reviewing what was planned last time, and end by setting a specific task for each person. Keep sessions to a sensible length, usually between 60 and 90 minutes, with a short break. Celebrate small wins, such as a topic everyone finally understands. If attendance drops, ask honestly what is getting in the way, and consider changing the time or format rather than just waiting. If the group is full of people who are no longer active, the owner can remove inactive members to make room for others who want to join.
