## Online tools and habits
For online sessions, choose one video call tool that everyone can join without installing anything difficult, and share the link in advance. Use a shared document or whiteboard so that everyone can see and edit the same working. Keep cameras on if people are comfortable, since seeing faces makes discussion easier, and mute microphones when not speaking to avoid background noise. Use the chat for links and short questions so that the speaker is not interrupted. Share your screen when explaining a problem, and slow down: it is harder to follow someone online than in a room. Test your connection and audio before the session starts. Record decisions and tasks in the shared notes so that anyone who missed the session can catch up.

## In-person venue, safety and accessibility
Choose a venue that is quiet, has enough table space and is easy for everyone to reach, such as a library study room, a campus common area or a quiet cafe. Check opening hours and whether you need to book a room. Think about accessibility: step-free access, good lighting and seating, and a space where people who need breaks or quiet can be comfortable. For safety, meet in public or well-used places, especially in the first few sessions with people you do not know yet, tell a friend where you are going, and travel home safely at a sensible hour. Agree beforehand how to share contact details, and only share what you are comfortable sharing. If anything makes you uncomfortable, you can leave, and you can speak to the group owner or leave the group.

## Keeping everyone involved
A good session is one where everybody talks, not just the most confident person. Start with a round where each person says one thing they found hard this week. Use turn-taking techniques, such as asking quieter members directly for their view or having everyone write an answer before anyone speaks. Rotate who leads each topic so that different people explain. Break larger groups into pairs for short exercises, then bring the pairs back together to share. Check in during long sessions with simple questions such as "Is the pace right?" and "Is anything unclear?". Hybrid groups, where some people are in the room and others join online, need extra care: make sure remote people can see the board and are invited to speak first, not last.
